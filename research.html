Syntax across the clause

Much of the syntactic work follows the cartographic approach and can be placed along the spine of the clause, from the discourse-related left periphery down to the verb phrase.

  1. CP

    Left periphery and discourse

    How information structure and speaker attitude are encoded at the top of the clause in Mandarin and Italian.

    • Topic and focus in Chinese
    • Exclamatives and mirativity
    • Discourse markers (你看 nǐ kàn, guarda te, allora)
    • Sentence-final particles
    • Politeness and the syntax of discourse
    • Peripheral adverbial clauses
    • Right dislocation
  2. IP

    The functional layer

    The inflectional domain: the hierarchy of aspectual and modal heads, subject clitics and auxiliaries.

    • Aspect markers in Mandarin
    • Modal verbs and necessity modals
    • Subject clitics in Venetan varieties
    • Auxiliary alternation (Venetan, Trentino, Sardinian)
  3. vP

    Low periphery and predication

    The area just above and inside the verb phrase: the low periphery and its focus positions, argument structure and copular predication.

    • The even-construction and preposed objects
    • Wh-questions and the low periphery
    • Causative and perception verbs (让 ràng)
    • Gei ‘give’
    • Verb–object constructions, cognate and dummy objects
    • Bare nouns
    • Copular and equative clauses (就是 jiù shì, 是 shì)

Other research areas

Syntax–prosody and syntax–pragmatics

How syntax interacts with intonation and discourse: focus in Italian echo wh-questions, interrogative right dislocation, and the prosody of discourse markers in spoken Italian.

  • Echo questions
  • Right dislocation
  • Turn-planning

Italian and its dialects

Microvariation in the dialects of Northern Italy and in regional Italian: reduplicated forms in Venetan (anca anca, gnanca gnanca), subject clitics, auxiliary selection, and discourse markers in regional Italian and Romanesco.

  • Venetan
  • Trentino
  • Romanesco
  • Italian–Chinese comparison

Acquisition, bilingualism and processing

First, second and heritage language acquisition of Chinese and Italian, and experimental work: aspect markers in L1 Mandarin, discourse markers in L2 and heritage Italian, sentence-final particles in L2 Mandarin, vocabulary assessment (LexITA), morphological processing and tone prediction in reading Chinese.

  • Heritage languages
  • L2 Italian and Chinese
  • Psycholinguistics
  • Pronunciation teaching

Documentation and typology

Documentation of Biyo, an endangered Lolo-Burmese language of China in contact with Southwestern Mandarin, and comparative work on Mandarin and Ewe (Kwa).

  • Biyo
  • Ewe
  • Analytic languages
